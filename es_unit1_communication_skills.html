Unit 1 — Overview
Communication Skills
Communication is a two-way process through which information or messages are exchanged between individuals. Effective communication helps us communicate the message correctly, precisely and completely.

Four Key Skills

  • Listening
  • Speaking
  • Reading
  • Writing

Elements of Communication

  • Sender (encodes message)
  • Channel (medium)
  • Receiver (decodes message)
  • Feedback (confirms understanding)

Ideal Message Qualities

  • Clear — conveys exact meaning
  • Concise — appropriately brief
  • Accurate — factually correct & verifiable

Session 1
Active Listening
Active listening is an art that comprises a desire to comprehend as well as offer support and empathy to the speaker. It allows you to understand problems and collaborate to develop solutions.
Note: Without the ability to listen attentively, messages can be easily misunderstood, communication breaks down, and the sender can become frustrated or irritated.

5 Stages of Active Listening

1
Receiving

Listening attentively — giving full, undivided attention.

2
Understanding

Comprehending the message being communicated.

3
Remembering

Retrieving or recalling information from what was heard.

4
Evaluating

Judging the value, importance and amount of what was said.

5
Responding

Saying or doing something as a meaningful response.

The RESPECT Model

R
Remove Distractions

Reduce the volume of television, radio or mobile phone while talking.

E
Eye Contact

Look at the speaker while listening — signals genuine attention.

S
Show You're Listening

Use gestures, nods, and open body language.

P
Pay Attention

Focus entirely on what the speaker is saying — not on planning your reply.

E
Empathise

Share the feelings of the speaker — imagine being in their situation.

C
Clarify Doubts

Ask polite, relevant questions to clear any misunderstandings.

T
Tune to Timing

Wait for the speaker to finish before responding — never interrupt.

Barriers to Active Listening

BarrierHow It Becomes a BarrierHow to Overcome It
Being Pre-occupiedEmotions take over; you may not listen carefullyDo not let emotions take over. Keep phones away.
Noise & Visual DistractionsYou may not hear the other person clearlyCreate a conducive environment.
Past Experiences / MindsetBiases distort what you hearAvoid biases; be objective in your approach.
Personal FactorsPersonal feelings about the speaker affect listeningAllow the other person to finish, then respond.

Parts of Speech
Parts of speech are categories of words based on their function within a sentence — the 'building blocks' of language.

Capitalisation Rules — MINTS

LetterRuleExample
M — MonthsCapitalise the first letter of all month namesI will go in June.
I — The letter 'I'Always capitalise 'I' when used as a wordEvery day, I play tennis.
N — NamesFirst letter of names of people, places, rivers, daysThis Tuesday, Vidya is in Rajasthan.
T — TitlesFirst letter of titles before people's namesDr Shah and Mr Patel work together.
S — Starting letterCapitalise the first letter of every sentenceThe cat ran out of the house.

Basic Parts of Speech

Part of SpeechWhat It DoesExample
NounNames a person, place, thing or ideaDog, India, Sanjay
PronounUsed in place of a nounI, They, He, You
AdjectiveDescribes a nounSmall, Blue, Sharp, Loud
VerbShows actionRun, Eat, Think, Sit
AdverbAdds meaning to a verb or adjective — answers how? when? where?Easily, Always, Inside, Before

Supporting Parts of Speech

📰

Articles

'a', 'an', 'the' — used before nouns. a book, an apple, the sun.

🔗

Conjunctions

Join nouns, phrases or sentences. Common: 'and', 'or', 'but'.

📍

Prepositions

Show relationships — where, when, how. Common: 'on', 'at', 'in', 'under'.

❗

Interjections

Express strong emotions with exclamation marks. Wow! Oh! Help!

Direct & Indirect Objects
The object of a sentence can be direct (answers "What?") or indirect (answers "To whom?" or "For whom?").
Direct only: Nisha sells a laptop. (What does Nisha sell? → a laptop)

Both: Abdul gave a gift to his mother. (What did he give? → a gift | To whom? → his mother)

Session 3 — Continued
Active & Passive Voice
In active voice, the subject does the action. In passive voice, the subject receives the action.
Active: Sanjay broke the glass. (subject does the action)
Passive: The glass was broken by Sanjay. (subject receives the action)
Active VoicePassive Voice
She wrote an email.An email was written by her.
Mohan sold a bike.The bike was sold by Mohan.
I did not beat him.He was not beaten by me.
Kapil made a profit yesterday.A profit was made by Kapil yesterday.
The tiger was chasing the deer.The deer was being chased by the tiger.
Session 3 — Continued
Types of Sentences
There are mainly four types of sentences — declarative, interrogative, exclamatory, and imperative. Each has a different purpose and punctuation.
📢

Declarative

States a fact. Ends with a full stop (.)
"I go to office."
"Reading mythology makes you more aware."

❓

Interrogative

Asks a question. Ends with a question mark (?)
"Did you complete your project?"
"How is the weather?"

😮

Exclamatory

Expresses strong emotion. Ends with an exclamation mark (!)
"Oh, it's very cold!"
"What a pleasant surprise!"

👉

Imperative

Shows command, request, or advice. Ends with (.) or (!)
"Wear your sweater."
"Get out of bed immediately!"

Session 3 — Continued
Writing Paragraphs
A group of sentences forms a paragraph. All sentences in a paragraph must share a common idea. Start a new paragraph for a new idea.
Example:

I go to Government Higher Secondary School, Balachadi. It is not a very big school but has many good teachers. There are about 100 students in my school. My school is on the main road, very close to the city railway station.

I love going to school and learning about new things. My school has a small playground, where I play cricket with my friends after classes. It has a library also. I love my school.
Tip: Practice writing paragraphs on topics you know well. Focus on using all four sentence types correctly and ensure each paragraph shares a single common idea.

Section D — Short Answer Questions (Q25–Q30)
Q25. What are the three qualities of an ideal message? Give an example of each.
(1) Clear — conveys the exact meaning. E.g., "Please allow me to reach office at 11 AM on Tuesday" vs the vague "Please allow me to reach late." (2) Concise — brief and to the point, e.g., replying "Alright, you may come late." (3) Accurate — factually correct and verifiable. E.g., "50 per cent of the books have been completed" vs the vague "most of the books."
Q26. What is the RESPECT model? Name and explain all seven steps.
R — Remove distractions (reduce TV/phone volume). E — Eye contact (look at the speaker). S — Show you are listening (gestures, nods). P — Pay attention (focus on what is being said, not your reply). E — Empathise (share the feelings of the speaker). C — Clarify doubts (ask polite, relevant questions). T — Tune to timing (wait for the speaker to finish before responding).
Q27. Write one sentence each for all four sentence types — declarative, interrogative, exclamatory, and imperative.
Declarative: I completed my project on time.
Interrogative: Did you complete your project?
Exclamatory: I completed my project!
Imperative: Complete your project today.
Q28. Explain the difference between a direct object and an indirect object with an example for each.
A direct object directly receives the action of the verb — it answers "What?". Example: "Nisha sells a laptop." — 'a laptop' is the direct object.

An indirect object answers "To whom?" or "For whom?". Example: "Abdul gave a gift to his mother." — 'a gift' is the direct object; 'his mother' is the indirect object.
Q29. What are the four barriers to active listening and how can each be overcome?
1. Being pre-occupied — Do not let emotions take over; keep phones away.
2. Noise and visual distractions — Create a quiet, conducive environment.
3. Past experiences/mindset — Avoid biases; be objective when interacting.
4. Personal factors — Allow the other person to finish speaking before you respond.
Q30. Convert these sentences: (a) Active to Passive: "Kapil made a profit yesterday."   (b) Passive to Active: "The email was written by her."
(a) Active → Passive: "A profit was made by Kapil yesterday."
The object 'a profit' becomes the subject; 'Kapil' follows 'by'; verb changes to 'was made'.

(b) Passive → Active: "She wrote the email."
The agent 'her' becomes 'she' (subject); 'The email' becomes the object; verb changes from 'was written' to 'wrote'.
