Unit 2 — Overview
Self-management Skills
Self-management, also referred to as 'self-control', is the ability to control one's emotions, thoughts and behaviour effectively in different situations. This includes motivating oneself and working towards achieving personal and academic goals.

What Self-management Helps With

  • Coming to class or work on time
  • Paying attention and maintaining discipline
  • Obeying teachers, parents and elders
  • Developing better study and work habits

Three Core Areas

  • Positive thinking — believe you can get things done
  • Result orientation — dream big and achieve set results
  • Self-awareness — know your strengths and personality

Session 1
Motivation
Motivation is derived from the word 'motive'. Directing behaviour towards a certain motive or goal is the essence of motivation. It may come from within (intrinsic) or be inspired by others or events (extrinsic).
"Optimism is the faith that leads to achievement. Nothing can be done without hope and confidence." — Helen Keller
💡

Intrinsic Motivation

Activities for which there is no apparent external reward — one derives enjoyment and satisfaction in doing them. The person is internally motivated because the activity brings pleasure or feels significant. Incentives related to the motive or goal can satisfy one's needs.

🏆

Extrinsic Motivation

Arises because of incentives or external rewards. Lack of motivation or incentives may lead to frustration — for example, employees kept on contractual basis for a long time may get frustrated and leave an organisation.

Session 1 — Continued
Positive Attitude
A positive attitude makes a person happier, helps build and maintain relationships, increases chances of success, and helps make better decisions. People who maintain a positive attitude in life situations are able to move forward more than those with a negative attitude.

Ways to Maintain a Positive Attitude

1
Start the Day Right

Begin with a morning routine — say positive affirmations, smile often, and think about the tasks to be accomplished during the day.

2
Feed the Mind with Positivity

Read motivating books, listen to uplifting music, and watch inspiring movies.

3
Be Proactive

A proactive person decides how they must feel regardless of what may be going on around them or what the day may bring.

4
Focus on Solutions, Not Problems

Do not approach life with 'problems'. Approach it with 'solutions' and constructive thinking.

5
Learn from Failures

Think what could have been better and work towards the goals instead of dwelling on the setback.

6
Focus on the Present

Negativity mostly stems from anxiety about past and future events. Stay in the present moment.

7
Move Towards Your Goals

Be cheerful and work hard to achieve your dreams with sustained effort and a positive mindset.

Long-term Techniques for a Positive Outlook

🏃

Physical Exercise & Fresh Air

Practise yoga, meditation and deep breathing. Playing in the park or taking a walk provides fresh air and makes you more active.

🥗

Healthy Diet

A balanced diet — daal, roti, green vegetables and fruits — provides the strength required to do daily work efficiently and supports a healthy mind.

📓

Organise Academic Life

Keep class notes organised, complete assignments on time, and track all deadlines to reduce stress and channel your mind towards goals.

😴

Adequate Sleep

At least seven hours of sleep is important so that the mind and body can get recharged to function better the next day.

🏖️

Holidays with Family & Friends

Visiting grandparents or a new place during vacation helps break the monotonous routine and come back refreshed.

Remember: Whether it's an exam result or a job interview rejection, maintaining a positive attitude and making persistent efforts allows all dreams to be achieved over time.
Session 1 — Continued
Stress and How to Manage It
Stress is a state of feeling upset, annoyed and hopeless. There are times when we feel nothing is working right and we are unable to achieve our goals. Managing and controlling emotions during such times helps us sail through.

Ways to Manage Stress

1
Stay Positive and Analyse the Situation

Understand what is going wrong. Resolving a situation is easy once it is clearly understood.

2
Maintain an Accomplishment Sheet

Enter even small achievements. Reviewing it whenever negative thoughts take over helps shift focus back to progress.

3
Keep Thoughts in the Present

Pondering over past issues makes us feel upset and helpless. Stay grounded in the current moment.

4
Talk to Friends and Family

Sharing feelings and seeking comfort from trusted people is one of the most effective stress-relief strategies.

5
Practise Meditation and Yoga

These practices calm the mind, improve circulation, and help restore a sense of balance and focus.

Key Insight: Managing stress effectively and maintaining a positive attitude helps one achieve career heights and maintain a healthy work–life balance.
Session 2
Result Orientation
Result orientation is the ability to recognise what results are important and the steps needed to achieve them. An ideal employee must be proactive and result-driven — always taking the necessary steps to achieve set goals or targets.
Key Idea: Result orientation describes an individual or organisation that focuses on outcome rather than the process used to produce a product or deliver a service.

How to Become Result-Oriented

i
Set Clear Goals

Setting clear and accurate goals is the first step needed to meet targets. Vague goals lead to vague results.

ii
Prepare an Action Plan

An action plan describes how a person or organisation will meet the set objectives. Each step should address: what changes will occur, who will carry them out, when, how long, what resources are needed, and who needs to be informed.

iii
Use the Right Resources and Tools

Evaluate what resources and tools are needed to achieve results and whether they are available. For example — do you have the right books to study for an entrance exam?

iv
Communicate with Mentors and Peers

Talk to teachers, seniors and mentors for help in setting realistic, achievable goals.

v
Make a Calendar

Create a calendar to monitor progress at regular intervals and stay on track.

vi
Work Hard

Work hard and believe in your dreams — consistent effort over time is what turns goals into achievements.

Examples of Result-Oriented Goals

Student

  • Scoring high marks in an exam
  • Completing all assignments before the deadline

Athlete

  • Running five miles a day
  • Improving personal best time in a race

Traveller

  • Reaching a destination city within three hours
Session 2 — Continued
Goal Setting — The SMART Framework
Goal setting helps us understand what we want, how to achieve it, and how we measure our success. Use the SMART acronym to set effective goals.
S
Specific

Goals should be stated in specific terms. Vague goals are difficult to attain. A specific goal gives a concrete target with a clear purpose.

M
Measurable

Goals should always be measurable. If we do not set goals in measurable terms, it is difficult to assess whether we have achieved them or not.

A
Action-oriented

Goals do not come true on their own. Effective goal setting must include action-based steps. Ask: What actions am I already taking? What will I start taking? Where do I need help?

R
Realistic

Goals must always be realistically attainable. Setting ourselves up for failure damages our sense of self-efficacy. Dream big, but ground goals in reality.

T
Timely

Goals must have deadlines. Deadlines may change, but one must always set a deadline to get the job done within a specified time limit.

Identifying a Goal — Key Questions:

• What is the goal about? (e.g., customer satisfaction, speeding up delivery)
• How would the goal make a difference? (e.g., enhance loyalty, increase demand for a product)
• Then apply SMART to finalise the goal statement.
Session 3
Self-awareness
Self-awareness is about understanding one's own needs, desires, habits, traits, behaviours and feelings. When we are not aware of ourselves, we move in a direction about which we are not clear — just like the rider who said "ask the horse".
The Monk Story:

A monk slowly walks along a road when he hears a galloping horse. He turns and sees a man riding fast in his direction. The monk asks, "Where are you going?" The man replies, "I don't know, ask the horse" — and rides away.

When we are not self-aware, we similarly move in a direction we are not clear about.

Three Steps Towards Self-awareness

1
Gain Awareness of Your Emotions

The first step is gaining a greater awareness of the emotions you experience — recognising what you feel and when.

2
Track Your Feelings

Make a habit of tracking your feelings regularly. Journaling or reflection exercises help identify patterns in emotional responses.

3
Expand to Broader Areas of Life

Go beyond just feelings — extend your self-awareness practice to relationships, habits, work, and how you relate to the world around you.

Session 3 — Continued
Personality and Personality Traits
Personality is a cluster of thoughts, feelings and behaviours that make a person unique and different from others. Personality traits are relatively lasting patterns of thoughts, feelings and behaviours that distinguish individuals from one another.

What Shapes Personality

  • Temperament and character
  • Environment and experiences
  • Culture and upbringing
  • Ongoing interactions between all the above

Why Personality Matters at Work

  • Affects performance and productivity
  • Shapes relationships with colleagues
  • A positive personality leads to cordial working relationships
  • Helps identify strengths and areas to grow
Session 3 — Continued
The Big Five Factors (FFM)
There are five parameters that describe an individual's personality. These are called the 'Big Five Factors' and the model is referred to as the Five Factor Model (FFM).
🌐

Openness

Creative, curious, active, flexible and adventurous. Interested in learning new things, meeting new people and visiting new places. Broad-minded in approach.

📋

Conscientiousness

Self-disciplined, completes work on time, takes care of others before themselves, and is guided by a strong inner conscience and concern for others' feelings.

🎉

Extraversion

Loves interacting with people, generally talkative, confident and outgoing. Can easily make friends and make any gathering lively and energetic.

🤝

Agreeableness

Generally kind, sympathetic, cooperative, warm and considerate. Accommodates themselves in any situation. Helps and takes care of others easily.

😟

Neuroticism

Tendency towards anxiety, self-doubt, depression, shyness and other negative feelings. People with high neuroticism often worry too much and find it hard to meet others.

Session 3 — Continued
Common Personality Disorders
Personality disorders involve long-term patterns of thoughts and behaviour that are unhealthy and rigid. They deviate from worldly expectations, cause distress, and persist over time. They are grouped into three clusters.
Cluster A

Suspicious

People in this cluster always mistrust others and are suspicious even when there is no reason to be. Example: Varsha has CCTV cameras at home, yet constantly follows and monitors the maid who has worked for two years — causing unnecessary stress in their relationship.

Paranoid Personality Disorder

Characterised by deep distrust of others, including friends, family and partners. People with this disorder mostly hold grudges against others.

Schizoid Personality Disorder

The person directs attention toward inner life and away from the external world. Appears detached, aloof and emotionally cold. Shows little interest in forming personal relationships.

Schizotypal Personality Disorder

People believe they can influence others or events with their thoughts. They often misinterpret behaviours, have inappropriate emotional responses, and consistently avoid intimate relationships.

Cluster B

Emotional and Impulsive

Characterised by unstable moods and behaviours leading to unhealthy relationships, emotional instability and feelings of worthlessness. Example: Sunny gets low marks, becomes depressed, fights with friends, doesn't learn from mistakes, and shouts at parents and teachers who try to help.

Antisocial Personality Disorder

Disregards social rules and obligations. Irritating, aggressive and impulsive. Tends to lack guilt and fails to learn from experience. May lie, steal, abuse, or become addicted to alcohol or drugs.

Borderline Personality Disorder

Lack of self-worth leading to feelings of emptiness and fears of abandonment. Pattern of emotional instability, violent outbursts and impulsive behaviour. Suicidal threats and self-harm may occur. Difficulty dealing with stressful events.

Histrionic Personality Disorder

Frequently tries to gain attention by being overly dramatic. Extremely sensitive to criticism or disapproval, and can be easily influenced by others.

Narcissistic Personality Disorder

Believes they are more important than others. Lacks empathy for other people and tends to exaggerate their own achievements and importance.

Cluster C

Anxious

Characterised by feelings of worry, anxiety or fear that affect daily routine. Example: Shikha washes her hands at least 20 times a day yet feels they are not clean. She neither talks to grandchildren nor participates in family activities.

Avoidant Personality Disorder

Feels socially inept, unappealing or inferior. Constantly fears being embarrassed, criticised or rejected. Avoids meeting others and experiences feelings of inadequacy and unattractiveness.

Dependent Personality Disorder

Lacks self-confidence and has an excessive need to be looked after. Needs help making everyday decisions and surrenders important life decisions to others. Heavily dependent on others emotionally and physically, and usually avoids being alone.

Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder

Strongly adheres to rules and regulations. Excessive concern with orderliness, perfectionism and attention to details. Feels extremely uncomfortable when unable to achieve perfection. May neglect personal relationships to focus on making something perfect.

Steps to Overcome Personality Disorders

1
Talk to Someone

Most often, it helps to share your feelings with a trusted person, friend, or counsellor.

2
Look After Your Physical Health

A healthy body helps maintain a healthy mind — exercise, sleep and diet all play a key role.

3
Build Confidence

Build confidence in your ability to handle difficult situations one step at a time.

4
Engage in Hobbies

Music, dance and painting have a therapeutic effect and provide healthy emotional outlets.

5
Stay Positive

Choose words like 'challenges' instead of 'problems' — language shapes mindset.

Section D — Short Answer Questions (Q25–Q28)
Q25. Differentiate between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation with one example each.
Intrinsic Motivation: Motivation that comes from within — the person does an activity because it brings them enjoyment, pleasure or satisfaction, with no apparent external reward. Example: A student reads extra books on history simply because they love learning about the past.

Extrinsic Motivation: Motivation that arises due to incentives or external rewards. Example: An employee works overtime because they will receive a bonus for doing so. Lack of such incentives can lead to frustration.
Q26. Explain the SMART framework for goal setting with one example of a SMART goal.
S — Specific: Goals must have a clear, concrete purpose — not vague.
M — Measurable: Goals must be stated in measurable terms so progress can be tracked.
A — Action-oriented: Goals must include specific action steps to be followed.
R — Realistic: Goals must be realistically attainable — setting up for failure is harmful.
T — Timely: Goals must have deadlines to ensure they are completed within a specified time.

Example of a SMART Goal: "I will score at least 80% in my quarterly Science exam by studying three hours daily and practising one sample paper every Sunday for the next four weeks."
Q27. Describe the Big Five Factors of personality with a one-line description of each.
1. Openness: Creative, curious, flexible and adventurous — interested in learning and new experiences.
2. Conscientiousness: Self-disciplined, works on time, guided by conscience and care for others.
3. Extraversion: Outgoing, talkative, confident and energised by social interaction.
4. Agreeableness: Kind, cooperative, warm and considerate — accommodates themselves in any situation.
5. Neuroticism: Tendency towards anxiety, self-doubt, depression and negative emotions.
Q28. Name the three clusters of personality disorders and give one example disorder for each cluster.
Cluster A — Suspicious: People in this cluster show deep mistrust and suspicion of others. Example: Paranoid Personality Disorder — distrust of family and friends, tendency to hold grudges.

Cluster B — Emotional and Impulsive: Characterised by unstable moods, unhealthy relationships and emotional instability. Example: Narcissistic Personality Disorder — believes they are more important than others; lacks empathy.

Cluster C — Anxious: Characterised by worry, anxiety and fear that affect daily routine. Example: Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder — excessive concern with orderliness and perfectionism; feels uncomfortable when perfection is not achieved.
